## Candidate contribution and honest gate

The defensible candidate is a complete, certified budget frontier for history-dependent sealed-block games, supported by common-update decomposition, exact counterfactual pricing, feasible schedule coarsening and cross-calendar opponent-response bounds. These building blocks specialize established minimax/oracle ideas. No individual monotonicity, branch-and-bound or column-generation fact is claimed new.

The engineering pilot agrees with the v13 endpoint values and makes the planned finite benchmark computationally plausible. Proceed to the approved development experiments to determine whether schedule selection, loss certificates and computation gains are substantial. This is permission to test a candidate method, not a passed Q1 novelty gate. Broader literature and final comparison continue before manuscript claims are finalized.

The full-text follow-up does not establish a new general game-solving theorem. Huang–Zhu's experiments illustrate one planar pursuer/evader with linear stochastic dynamics and analytically separated observation/control choices. Our command-revision budget differs from paying to acquire noisy state information; neither model subsumes the other without changing information semantics. Before the frozen test, the defensible method question is whether the specialized combination gives useful certified budget decisions. If deletion certificates remain loose or calendar pruning adds no meaningful savings, report that finding and narrow the paper's contribution. No outcome-dependent alteration of the frozen physical design is authorized by this assessment.
